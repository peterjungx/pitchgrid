# Philosophy of PitchGrid

*Notes toward a manifesto — Peter Jung, February 2026*

---

## The Core Insight

**The tonal structure of Western music is two-dimensional.**

This is not a new invention. It is THE crucial insight — the conceptual leap that makes a new world accessible.

Thinking about interval relationships in one dimension, as we have practiced until now, limits our understanding. It operates in a projected space. It is this implicit two-dimensionality, opening it back up, unfolding it again, that gives us a new perspective.

The stack of fifths construction known from Babylonian times is the origin of the diatonic scale, and that construction is intrinsically two-dimensional. So the PitchGrid concept is well rooted in history.

We can better understand:
- How the diatonic scales emerge
- How the modes emerge
- How different keys in the spiral of fifths work
- What transposition really entails

...by looking at musical structure from this two-dimensional perspective.

**The tonal structure of Western music is — and always was — two-dimensional.**

PitchGrid is a natural continuation of Western musical culture.

---

## The Dead Ends of the 20th Century

### Atonality and Serialism

The obsession with "democratizing the 12 notes" by Stockhausen, and all atonal music from the 20th century, was a dead end. Much of it is just experimentation with the possibilities electronics gives us in terms of sound production. It never gained widespread popularity. Pop music still is 99.9% the diatonic scale.

### Free Jazz

Free-Jazz enthusiasts obsess in avoiding any clichés. It's a dead end — most listeners *want* those clichés. PitchGrid allows you to hear those clichés within a completely new tonal architecture, adding novelty and excitement. That's what people want.

### Microtonalists and Just Intonation

Microtonal enthusiasts obsess with just ratios, regularity being an afterthought, and in practice mostly projecting to an EDO anyway.

Other microtonalists obsess with adding new frequencies, and inventing new notations basically projecting higher dimensionalities and messing up the notation system, which adds complexity and hinders adoption. This is a dead end as well.

If they would embrace the true two-dimensional nature of our music theory, which is rather explicit in our notation system (scale degree being one dimension, accidentals the other), and if they would embrace the possibilities this offers, maybe they would not feel the urge to add new types of accidentals all the time, obfuscating what our notation system really tells us.

### The EDO Universalists

I have read about scholars debating about what subdivision of the octave should be used to have a universal basis for any music — should it be 60EDO or 72EDO?

This approach is misguided because of the bandwidth limit of the human mind, which naturally limits the number of scale degrees we pick for any singable and well-memorable melodic line. For any piece, we will pick our 5-7 pitches, as do all the cultures before us.

## What Is Universal vs. What Is 12-TET Specific

PitchGrid requires us to re-evaluate the notions we were taught to be universal, to identify those that are truly universal and abandon those that really are only valid in 12-TET.

### Truly Universal

- Modes
- Keys
- Notation (scale degree + accidentals)
- Functional tonality
- Principles of voice leading and counterpoint

### 12-TET Specific (to abandon)

- Circle of fifths consisting of 12 degrees
- The concept of the semitone as fundamental unit
- Conflating small scale degree with accidental

---

## Why 12-TET Won (And What It Teaches Us)

The success of 12-TET is basically proof that **regular structure is more important than justness of the tuning**.

The success of the piano is due to:

1. **A unified framework** — a closed system of thinking
2. **Practical cost advantages** — it is much cheaper to build an organ with 12 keys per octave than 31 keys, even if the latter would have superior sound
3. **Human bandwidth** — the idea that there are 12 fixed notes we need to deal with frees the mind from having to deal with tuning at all

## The Marketing Challenge

When marketing PitchGrid, we should be very aware of this drive to simplify.

We necessarily add complexity, and anyone embracing it has to pay the price of extra processing cycles required to learn new concepts. We need to convince the visitor of the website that it is worth it:

- It opens up new sonic possibilities
- It is a path forward for Western music
- It does not make away with the concepts we learned, but deepens their understanding

---

## The Target Audience

The target group is young, hungry, talented musicians, eager to discover what the possibilities are.

Very many young people embracing microtonality are basically stuck, because the language and whole approach of the microtonalists is so disconnected from Western music theory. I have met quite some who are frustrated, who feel that they wasted time learning just intonations and entered the jungle of the Xenharmonics. Their music lacks overall architecture, which Western music theory used to give them.

This is something that PitchGrid CAN offer.

Maybe, with PitchGrid, Jacob Collier would not have turned his back to microtonal music to create purely diatonic songs again. Maybe all the great musicians who, in their early years, also said "there is more," but later on gave up and returned to the 12 tones, would have made significant contributions in alternative tunings — if they had seen the two-dimensional structures made explicit by PitchGrid.

**This is the promised land.**

---

## The Honest Gap: No Corpus Yet

What PitchGrid can't offer (and the Xenharmonic community can't offer, either) is a corpus.

This is maybe, after I have fleshed out the PitchGrid concept, the single biggest constraint. There is no memory of melodies in the non-traditional intonations that PitchGrid gives access to. They do not yet exist. There is no culture yet having embraced these new tonalities.

---

## The Historical Moment

The obstacle for musicians was mental and practical.

Now, with digital technology, we are able to easily create any tuning system we desire and consistently and logically lay them out on isomorphic controllers, which frees us from the piano layout.

The situation has changed compared to a couple of decades ago, where we would have needed to build new instruments for every intonation — a big practical obstacle.

Now we can enter different realms by the turn of a knob, scales automatically re-mapped.

**The practical obstacle is gone. The only obstacle left is mental.**

---

## The Promise

PitchGrid is the map that frustrated microtonalists didn't have. The architectural framework they lost when they left Western theory behind.

Western theory *extended*, not abandoned.

The promised land exists — but it's empty, waiting for settlers. There are no standards yet. The first great works in these tunings haven't been written.

Maybe you'll write them.
